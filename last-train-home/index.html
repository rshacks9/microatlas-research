What it does

Tracks your walk on the Monon, snapped to the trail so GPS wobble doesn't pad your miles. A conductor calls every stop with a bit of history, times your splits, and knows when you've turned around.

The person waiting at home gets an arrivals board with where you are and when you’ll be home, and can send telegrams that the conductor reads aloud.

About

How it works

Tracking

GPS points are matched to the trail itself, so distance on the Monon is measured along the path and stray points get thrown out. Off the trail, points are smoothed before they count. The app knows the trail from 10th Street to 146th Street in Carmel, and any spots you name along it.

Phones pause web pages when the screen locks. Keep the screen on (the app asks for that) and use Pocket mode. If tracking does pause, the gap is measured along the trail when you come back.

Home time

The home estimate uses your recent pace, stops included, and the distance left: back along the trail, then the same way home you came. Before you turn around it assumes your chosen turnaround, then your usual one.

Put it on your phone

The preview inside Claude can't use GPS. Host the index.html file on any free static host, then add it to your Home Screen.

What's new

  • Alerts and the last update of a walk keep trying until they land, and the pickup card says whether her phone got the alert.
  • Heading home by the streets ends the walk at your door, and a pause no longer looks like a long stop.
  • Her board tells a walk that ended away from home apart from one that ended at home, and shows last night's walk as history.
  • Resuming a walk after a reload no longer reads old telegrams aloud, and an unfinished walk is saved before a new one starts.
Earlier updates
  • A Departures board on the depot: tonight's turn, the round trip, and when you'd be home if you left now. All aboard stays in reach, and a home-by time gets a yes or a nearer stop.
  • A red enamel station sign, one instrument cluster, and clear On and Off controls in the cab. The sign says if you walk past your turnaround.
  • Pocket mode shows a countdown, a progress rail, her latest telegram and any check-in, in a red you can actually read.
  • Her board puts the telegram and the guess near the top, groups alerts in one card, and her intro sets up sounds and alerts.
  • Ticket book tabs and month headings, records that open their walk, and a share picture with a tear-off stub.
  • Four voices: Conductor, Coach, Historian and Night Owl, each with its own check-ins.
  • Weather, rain warnings, sunset and the moon phase, plus a Dispatch card before you leave.
  • A live map and a trip chart on the arrivals board, cheers she can send, and a heads-up time she picks.
  • A check-in after ten minutes stopped, with an alert to the board if there's no answer.
  • The whole Indianapolis trail from 10th Street, your own named spots, and a sheet for every stop.
  • Plan by distance or time, walk details with a route map, shareable ticket pictures, and weekly charts.
  • Retries when the signal drops, a custom relay option, backups, a first-walk checklist and a big view.
  • Race a ghost of your best walk, earn merit badges, get a Monday recap, and send quick replies from the cab.
  • Her board adds tonight's timeline, a guess-his-arrival game he hears about, and a QR code for the link.

Voices

Four voices share one brain. The Conductor calls stations with a bit of history. The Coach watches your pace, compares miles, and nudges you when you fade. The Historian tells more stories. The Night Owl keeps quiet, checks in every half hour, and mentions the moon and sky.

Weather and sky

Weather comes from Open-Meteo and refreshes every 30 minutes on a walk, with rain warnings when a shower looks likely within the hour. Sunset and the moon phase are calculated on this phone. The moon phase is the average cycle, so it can be off by up to a day.

Privacy

Your walks, history and settings stay on this phone. Five services see a little, and only when used:

  • ntfy.sh carries live board updates, encrypted on this phone first. The key lives only in the board link, so anyone with the link can see your walk: send it to one person. The relay can see when updates are sent and from which internet address, but not what they say. Phone alerts, if she turns them on, are plain text with status, times and the nearest cross street, never your exact position.
  • Open-Meteo gets your location rounded to about a kilometer, for the weather on the Depot and during walks.
  • CARTO serves the map pictures and sees which part of town is on screen.
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim gets the address you type, only if you search for your home.
  • Google Fonts serves the typefaces, so Google sees this page’s internet address when they load.

Trail hours

Carmel posts its Monon Greenway, north of 96th Street, as open sunrise to sunset. The conductor mentions it when you reach 96th after dark.
